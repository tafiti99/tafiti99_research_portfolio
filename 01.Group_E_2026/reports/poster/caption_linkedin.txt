📊 What truly drives modern contraceptive uptake in East Africa? Real evidence from 419 women in Mkuranga District, Tanzania.

When designing reproductive health programs, we often assume method awareness is the biggest bottleneck. But our recent cross-sectional epidemiological investigation revealed a far more nuanced—and actionable—clinical reality.

Here are the key findings from our study of 419 women of reproductive age (15–49 years):

1️⃣ Current Prevalence: 25.1% (95% CI: 21.1% – 29.4%)
Among all women, 1 in 4 currently uses a modern contraceptive method, rising to 28.1% among currently married or cohabiting women. The predominant method choice remains injectable contraception (45.7%), followed by subdermal implants (27.6%) and oral pills (14.3%).

2️⃣ The Knowledge Paradox:
Awareness of at least one method was high—90.7% recognized injectables. However, comprehensive knowledge across mechanisms, side effects, and advantages averaged just 9.6 out of 18 points. Crucially, multivariable regression showed that women with high contraceptive knowledge were nearly twice as likely to use modern contraception (adjusted Prevalence Ratio [aPR] = 1.89, 95% CI: 1.35–2.65, P < 0.001).

3️⃣ Male Partner Involvement is the Decisive Driver:
Contrary to common assumptions that family planning decisions are made independently, partner dynamics emerged as the strongest modifiable predictors:
• Women who discussed family planning with their partners frequently had 2.7 times higher contraceptive uptake (aPR = 2.67, 95% CI: 1.57–4.52, P < 0.001).
• Even occasional communication (once or twice) more than doubled uptake (aPR = 2.39, 95% CI: 1.44–3.99, P < 0.001).
• Having a supportive partner independently increased prevalence by 85% (aPR = 1.85, 95% CI: 1.14–3.01, P = 0.013).

4️⃣ The High-Parity Paradox:
Counter-intuitively, women with ≥3 living children had 32% lower contraceptive prevalence than women with 0–2 children (aPR = 0.68, 95% CI: 0.47–0.98, P = 0.040). This highlights an urgent need to address contraceptive fatigue, fear of side effects, and cultural perceptions of family completion in multiparous women.

5️⃣ A Methodological Note on Biostatistics:
Because the outcome prevalence was 25.1% (well above the 10% threshold), traditional logistic regression would have severely overestimated the true effect sizes (Odds Ratio inflation). We implemented Modified Poisson regression with robust Huber-White sandwich variance (Zou, 2004) in R, ensuring mathematically sound, directly interpretable Prevalence Ratios.

💡 Key Policy & Clinical Takeaway:
If we want to raise modern contraceptive prevalence, programs must transition from female-only clinic visits to couple-centered interventions that actively empower male partners as supportive co-decision-makers.
