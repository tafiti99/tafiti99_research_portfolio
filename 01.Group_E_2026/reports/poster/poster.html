WHAT TRULY DRIVES CONTRACEPTIVE UPTAKE?

Real-World Evidence from 419 Women of Reproductive Age in Mkuranga District

In this cross-sectional study of 419 women, modern contraceptive prevalence was 25.1%. Using Modified Poisson regression (Zou 2004) to avoid Odds Ratio inflation, we evaluated independent knowledge and socio-cultural determinants.

THREE CRITICAL LESSONS FOR CLINICIANS & RESEARCHERS

1. ENGAGE PARTNERS ACTIVELY

Partner communication and approval were the single strongest drivers of modern contraceptive uptake (aPR up to 2.67). Interventions must engage couples rather than treating family planning as female-only.

2. TARGET HIGH-PARITY BARRIERS

Women with 3 or more children showed 32% lower use despite having achieved family size goals. Reproductive health services must proactively target misconceptions and fatigue in multiparous women.

3. USE MODIFIED POISSON IN R

Because prevalence was 25.1% (>10%), logistic regression Odds Ratios would severely overestimate true risk. Modified Poisson regression (log-link + sandwich::vcovHC) guarantees valid Prevalence Ratios.
